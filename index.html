Practice

Practice areas

From single studies to recurring reports, built on each firm's own records.

I.

Consolidation

Most firms keep the same project, client or job in several places: the accounting system, one or more client databases, and spreadsheets kept by individual staff. We match these records, resolve the differences, and maintain a single consistent source that updates automatically.

Typical outputs: a reconciliation of every open project across systems; a list of missing or conflicting records with the value at stake; a clean, connected source for reporting.

II.

Recurring reports

Reports are issued on a fixed schedule, drawn directly from the client's systems and checked against the books before issue. They cover what owners and finance leads need each week: cash position, receivables, work not yet invoiced, and profitability by job, client or line of work.

Typical outputs: a weekly position; a monthly profitability report; a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast; alerts when a figure moves outside agreed limits.

III.

Analysis

Fixed-scope studies answer a single question with the firm's own history. The most common is pricing: comparing estimates with actual costs across past jobs to find the kinds of work that are consistently under- or over-priced.

Typical outputs: estimate accuracy by type of work; margin by client and sector; return on each source of new business; a written finding with recommended changes.

IV.

Operational systems

We build systems for recurring administrative work and for access to the firm's project history. They draw on the same consolidated records as the reports.

  • Project archive. Past projects indexed by building type, material, treatment, client and outcome, for reference in pricing and proposals.
  • Estimate verification. Estimates recorded in the CRM compared with their supporting documents, with discrepancies referred for review.
  • Proposal materials. Qualification and past-performance sections prepared from the archive for staff review.
  • Scheduled tasks. Invoice and retainage notices, change-order tracking and owner statements.
  • Field records. Applications for site staff to record hours, materials and photographs against a project.

Where possible, systems are built on software the firm already licenses, and are maintained under the monthly engagement.

Engagement

Assessment, build, operation

Engagements follow the same three stages.

Each begins with one question about the records, answered before anything is built.

1

Assessment

A review of operations and data sources, concluding with the questions the reporting is to answer.

2

Build

Read-only connection to existing systems and delivery of the initial reports and systems.

3

Operation

Reports issued on schedule and systems maintained. Changes in source systems are addressed as they occur. A named principal is responsible for the engagement.

Terms

Standing terms of every engagement

  • Read-only access. Connections are made through each system's authorized access. Shared passwords are not used.
  • Reconciled before issue. Every report is checked against the books; a failed check holds the report.
  • Data kept where agreed. Records stay in one secured environment, or inside the client's own systems, and are deleted at the end of the engagement.
  • A named principal. One principal is responsible for each engagement.
  • Owned by the client. Everything we build is documented and remains the firm's.

Questions

Common questions

How does this differ from our accountant?

An accountant keeps the books correct and files on time. We work from those books and from the operational records around them, such as estimates, jobs and timesheets, and issue reports that join the two. Many engagements run alongside the firm's accountant.

Why not use AI tools ourselves?

For a single question, they serve well. Recurring reporting needs connections that keep working, figures checked against the books every time, and someone responsible when a source system changes. That is the service.

Where do our records go?

Access is read-only. Records are held in one secured environment, or inside the firm's own systems, and deleted at the end of the engagement. Where AI tools are used, the terms exclude training on client data.

How is the work priced?

The assessment is a fixed fee, set once the scope is known. Ongoing work is a fixed monthly fee. There is no hourly billing.

How does an engagement start?

With one question about the firm's records, examined in the assessment. The written finding states what can be answered, from which records, and what it would take.

What happens if we stop?

Everything we build is documented and remains the firm's. Connections are handed over or closed, and any records we hold are deleted.
